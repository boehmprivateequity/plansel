Chapter 3 · The Portuguese grape varieties

Touriga Franca (R)

Origin:

Northern Portugal, crossing Marufo with Touriga Nacional. The name Touriga Francesa originated in the 1940s but had no relation to a French grape variety, according to Truel (1983/4). The current name comes from the official list of grape varieties, published in Ministerial Order 428/2000. Growing throughout the country. Region of greatest expansion: Douro. It is the most popular grape variety in the region.

Wine description:

The aroma is generally intense, with an obvious floral touch of roses and a fruity aroma of cistus only in good years. It’s a wine with well-balanced tannins and fruit, but without being exceptional. The aftertaste is quite fruity and persistent. It fills the mouth well, giving the sensation of a full-bodied wine with good structure, but with little elegance (Almeida, 1990/98, Douro).

Regional classification:

DOC quality wine: Porto, Douro, Távora-Varosa, Bairrada, Óbidos, Alenquer, Arruda, Torres Vedras, Tejo, Lagoa, Alentejo, Valpaços, Planalto Mirandês.

Regional wine: Minho, Trás-os-Montes, all sub-regions of Beira Interior, Estremadura province, Tejo, Terras do Sado, Alentejo, Algarve.

Morphology:

Young shoot (form of tip): Open, with medium anthocyanin colouration and sparse hairs.

Young leaf: Copper, with medium to strong starker anthocyanin colouration. Hairs of medium density.

Young shoot (form of tip): Red stripes on dorsal side of nodes and at internodes. Low anthocyanin colouration in buds. Short tendrils.

Inflorescence (sex of flower): Hermaphrodite.

Mature leaf: Medium to large, pentagonal, normally three lobes; deep green, rounded shape, medium blistering of upper side of blade, generally undulate. Short, convex teeth. Closed V-shaped, sometimes slightly overlapping, petiolar sinus closed. Open V-shaped upper lateral sinuses. Medium density prostrate hairs on lower side of blade.

Bunch: Medium and dense. Short peduncle, good lignification.

Berry: Medium, round oblate, blue-black. Skin furry, medium firmness. Flesh colourless, firm, very juicy. Short pedicel.

Phenology:

Time of bud burst: 1 day after the Castelão.

Flowering: Early, 1 day after the Castelão.

Berry (colour change): Early, 11 days before the Castelão.

Berry (harvest ripe): Normal, 1 week after the Castelão.

Vegetative and viticultural potential:

Vigour of shoot growth: Medium to high.

Pruning weight: 2,000–3,000 kg/ha.

Shoot attitude: Upright, Heliotropic. Some terminal shoots may develop a strong lateral habit and become very long.

Length of internodes: Medium.

Shoot length: Uniformly medium to large.

Tendency to form lateral shoots: Medium.

Rate of multiple bud bursts: Short and thin.

Bud fertility index: Low, between 1.5 and 1.7 flowers per shoot.

Yield: High (8,000–16,000 kg/ha). RNSV statistical value: 2.6 kg/vine (Average of at least 40 cl. in Vila Flor/ 4 years).

Yield consistency: Consistent.

Crop uniformity: Uniform.

Winkler Index: 1,496 for an 11 t/ha yield (Montemor). Because of its long growing season, desired maturity is achieved even in warm areas. Bad weather conditions in September even in warm areas can compromise yield.

Sensitivity to abiotic factors: Robust variety.

Susceptibility to fungal diseases: Somewhat susceptible to Oidium and sour rot, particularly when it has suffered from grapevine moth.

Training system: It adapts well to any training system, even with short pruning, as the yield is always satisfactory. Due to its erect size, it is easy to manage in green (mechanical pruning).

Favourable soil for quality: Avoid very fertile, deep and humid soils, which compromise the alcoholic strength.

Favourable climate: Preferably at low altitudes with good exposure, dry and warm to the South, SW and West quadrants, at an elevation of 300 m, as it withstands strong radiation well.

Vine density: Due to its vegetative habits, it adapts well to the vine spacing inherent to different forms of training and training systems.

Leaf blight: Less sensitive to leaf blight than Touriga Nacional.

Bunch preservation after ripening: Relatively high.

Oenology

Wine type: Quality wine, dessert wine, rosé wine.

Potential alcohol content: Medium (12–14% vol.); Low in humid zones. (RNSV statistical value: 11.35% vol. (Average of at least 40 clones in Tabuaço over 5 years).

Natural acidity of must: Medium (3.5– 4.5 g/l tartaric acid). RNSV statistical value: 3.94 g/l (Average of at least 40 clones in Tabuaço over 5 years).

Total anthocyanins: RNSV statistical value: 726.67 mg/l (Average of at least 40 clones in Tabuaço over 5 years).

Total phenols index (at 280 nm) of must: 78.8. RNSV statistical value: 31.33 (Average of 40 clones in Tabuaço 5 years).

Risk of oxidation of must: High.

Colour intensity of wine: 5.8–8.15.

Wine colour tonality: 0.66–0.73. Light reddish, somewhat brownish.

Total polyphenol index (at 280 nm): 22–25.

Risk of oxidation of wine: Tendency towards slight oxidation of wine,

Ageing potential: Generally good, particularly well suited to barrel ageing.
