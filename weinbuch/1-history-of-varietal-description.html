Chapter 3 · The Portuguese grape varieties

1. History of Varietal Description

“Descrição do terreno duas léguas em roda da cidade de Lamego” by Rui Fernandes in 1532 is the first known Portuguese description of grape varieties. In 171,2 Vinzenso Alarte followed with“Agricultura das Vinhas”, Lisbon, Oficina Real Deslandesiana. From 1790 to 1792, various authors published “Memória sobre a Cultura das Vinhas, e Manufactura do Vinho. Memórias da Agricultura”, Part II (1), Real Academia das Ciências de Lisboa, which provides a wide range of information on existing grape varieties. Many ampelographic works appeared in the 19th century before the onset of the “American Plagues” and almost countless works after the Phylloxera infestations of the vineyards.

The wines of the different areas were evaluated in relation to the varieties from which they were made. Vila Maior in his “Manual de Viticultura Prática” (Manual of Practical Viticulture) (1875: 430–515) and Ferreira Lapa in his Tecnologia Rural” (Rural Technology) (2nd edition, 1874: 92) summarised the ampelographic knowledge gained and came up with 220 different Portuguese varieties at the time.

The Ministry of Agriculture devoted itself tirelessly to the subject and published the results of the ampelographic studies in the Ministry’s bulletin, the “Boletim da Direcção Geral da Agricultura”. In this bulletin, Meneses’ catalogue of grape varieties (1896/1900) lists 320 references for different grape varieties up to the end of the 18th century, with a further 100 recorded up to 1850. By the end of the century, ampelography reached a peak on the international stage, with the work of the Frenchmen Vialat and Valmorel, who described 5,200 varieties. In Portugal, the work of Cincinnato da Costa (1900) “Portugal Vinícola” was particularly noteworkthtynoteworthy. However, reference should also be made to the “Ampelographic Studies” by Marques de Carvalho (1903), collaborator of the important ampelography of Vialat and Valmorel. At that time, the Academy of Sciences did not have sufficient financial means to publish de Carvalho’s book (Ref. internal note of the Academy, according to Telles Antunes). In this context, José de Lencastre (1945) since he produced a catalogue of viticultural publications containing some 3,305 works.

In the 1980s, the first systematic work to appear was João Antero Araújo’s dissertation on the varieties of the Alentejo. In parallel, a simplified inventory of the 341 different“official”grapevine varieties, including photographs and OIV-compliant descriptions, was made under the direction of the IVV (Instituto da Vinha e do Vinho – Institute of Vine and Wine) for all wine-growing regions in Portugal. Under the responsibility of the ampelographer of the EVN (Dois Portos), Dr. Eiras Dias, a catalogue of synonyms was compiled. The two exercises formed the basis for the grapevine variety register (Ministerial Order/Portaria n.º 428/2000 of 17 July) created in 2000, once the number of synonyms had been reduced and many homonymous designations had been eliminated. In the meantime, under a 2005 project on molecular analysis, all varieties were profiled and 17 synonyms identified. In 2007, Hans Jörg Böhm as co-author and coordinator, published ”Portugal Vitícola – O Grande Livro das Castas” (Viticultural Portugal – The Great Book of Grapevine Varieties). It describes 70 grape varieties in detail according to all known criteria, including geographical relevance (IVV). The new common organization of the markets (single CMO) for viticultural products−Article 81(2) of Regulation (EU) No 1308/2013 of the European Parliament and of the Council−made it compulsory for member states to classify grape varieties for wine production, determining that only these may be planted, replanted and grafted.

The significant number of traditional synonyms used for the same grape variety, based on the results of microsatellite analysis of these grape varieties, has limited the number of grape varieties authorized for wine production. These grape varieties, along with foreign grape varieties authorized by the IVV, are now included in the list of grape varieties suitable for wine production, under Ministerial Order 380/2012. All these grape varieties are planted in a collection at the Agricultural Research Institute at INIAV/ Dois Portos. This list is now of responsibility of the IVV, I.P., after assessing the submissions by the certifying bodies, associations and federations representing the wine sector, in accordance with Article 3(1) of the aforementioned Ministerial Order.

Besides the IVV list of grape varieties, parallel documentation exists in the form of the Catálogo Nacional de Variedades de videira (National Catalogue of Vine Varieties) – CNV, which contains a list of cultivars authorised by the plant protection services for propagation.
